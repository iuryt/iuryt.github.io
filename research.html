Research

From data, to dynamics, to impacts

My research sits at the intersection of physical oceanography, data science, and high-performance computing. It is organized around three connected lines: building the data and methods to observe the ocean across scales, using them to understand multiscale physical processes, and tracing how those processes affect ecosystems and human populations.

1 · Data-driven
Methods to fuse, share, and learn from large environmental datasets

2 · Dynamics
Multiscale physical processes in the ocean and atmosphere

3 · Impacts
How multiscale physics affects ecosystems and people

Data-driven methods

I develop methods and open-source tools to fuse observations and model output across platforms and scales, distribute them as open, analysis-ready datasets, and extract insight from them using high-performance and cloud computing, statistics, machine learning, and scalable visualization.

Global eddy–profile database

Merging 4.2 million vertical profiles with 35 million altimetry-tracked eddy observations.
Multiplatform ocean observations

Combining satellites, aircraft, and autonomous platforms into a consistent view of the upper ocean.
Open, cloud-native data tools

Software and workflows that make large ocean datasets fast to access and easy to explore.

Multiscale ocean & atmosphere dynamics

I use observations, theory, and numerical simulations to understand physical processes from the mesoscale down to the submesoscale, and their coupling with the atmosphere.

Mesoscale and submesoscale vortices

How ocean vortices form, evolve, interact with topography, and move water around.
Submesoscale fronts and mixed layer eddies

GPU-accelerated simulations of fronts, instabilities, and restratification of the upper ocean.
Air–sea interaction and cold pools

Atmospheric cold pools, air–sea fluxes, and monsoon variability in the Bay of Bengal.
